# UK Gas Balance and Physical Optionality

## Purpose

This project studies UK and European gas markets through physical balance, forward curves, storage, logistics and market risk.

The aim is not to build a trading system. The aim is to show the research process behind identifying where physical tightness, regional spreads, storage value or logistics constraints could create pricing pressure, supply stress or possible areas of value.

By optionality, I mean the value created by flexibility: the ability to store, move, delay, redirect or use gas differently when prices or physical conditions change.

In physical commodity markets, useful signals rarely come from one dataset. This project applies that logic using public sources: flows, inventories, prices, curves, infrastructure, policy and market commentary.

## Core market question

Is the UK gas market tightening or loosening, and what do storage levels, LNG send-out, interconnector flows, NBP/TTF spreads and forward curve structure suggest about physical market risk?

## Why this market matters

UK gas is a useful case study because it links LNG imports, European pipeline flows, storage, weather demand, power market stress and regional price spreads.

In physical commodity markets, value often comes from matching supply, timing, location and credit. A cargo, storage slot or flexible contract can be worth more when another market is short, infrastructure is constrained, or a buyer pays for reliability, timing or delivery flexibility.

## Physical commodity lens

This project is organised around six practical questions:

1. **Balance:** Is the system loose or tight?
2. **Curve:** Is the forward curve pricing seasonal scarcity or storage value?
3. **Spread:** Is UK gas cheap or expensive relative to continental Europe?
4. **Logistics:** Are LNG terminals, interconnectors or storage assets creating bottlenecks?
5. **Optionality:** When prices, weather or flows change, which assets become more valuable: storage, LNG flexibility, interconnectors or gas-fired generation?
6. **Risk:** What would change the interpretation, such as cold weather, low wind, weak LNG arrivals, Norwegian flow disruption or policy shocks?

## Signal interpretation table

| Signal | Loose market indication | Tight market indication |
|---|---|---|
| Storage | High and rising storage | Low storage or rapid withdrawals |
| LNG send-out | Strong send-out adding supply | Weak send-out or limited arrivals |
| Interconnector flows | Imports into the UK or balanced flows | Exports from the UK during domestic tightness |
| Weather demand | Mild conditions | Cold conditions and rising heating demand |
| Gas for power | Low gas burn | High gas burn due to low wind or high residual demand |
| NBP vs TTF | UK discount to continental Europe | UK premium or narrowing discount |
| Forward curve | Flat curve or comfortable summer/winter spread | Winter premium, backwardation or steep seasonal spread |
| Infrastructure | Normal terminal and pipeline availability | Outages, congestion or limited flexibility |

## Data and source discipline

Each dataset should be recorded with its source, frequency, transformation and limitation.

| Data area | Example source | Frequency | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gas prices | ICE, market price references or public market snapshots | Daily or monthly | Live exchange data may require paid access |
| Storage | GIE AGSI, National Gas or official storage publications | Daily or weekly | UK storage is small compared with continental European storage |
| LNG send-out | National Gas, terminal data or public reports | Daily or weekly | Terminal-level data may be incomplete |
| Interconnector flows | National Gas, ENTSOG or operator publications | Daily | Nominations and actual flows may differ |
| Weather demand | Met Office, degree-day proxies or weather datasets | Daily or weekly | Weather is only one demand driver |
| Power data | NESO, Elexon or public power data | Half-hourly, daily or monthly | Some data requires cleaning before use |
| Strategic risk | Government, regulator, company reports and news | Event-based | Qualitative judgement is required |

## First version outputs

The first version of this study focuses on four outputs:

1. **Gas balance table:** a structured view of supply, demand and net tightness.
2. **NBP/TTF spread note:** a short interpretation of UK gas value relative to continental Europe.
3. **Forward curve note:** a basic interpretation of seasonal scarcity and storage value.
4. **Methodology and source table:** a transparent record of sources, assumptions and limitations.

## Analyst update structure

Each update should answer six questions:

1. What changed?
2. Which indicator moved?
3. Why does it matter physically?
4. How could it affect prices, spreads or storage value?
5. What is uncertain?
6. What should be watched next?

## Limitations

This is a student research portfolio built from public data. It does not include proprietary trader information such as live bids and offers, private counterparty behaviour, internal inventory data, credit lines, freight negotiations or physical access to terminals and storage.

The purpose is not to claim a trading edge. The purpose is to show a disciplined approach to physical commodity market research using public sources, transparent assumptions and clear interpretation.
